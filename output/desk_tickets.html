Reflection

1. How I would evaluate the agents on each ticket

For ticket 101, I think the agents did the actual investigation well, but didn't finish the status update correctly. Boss sent it to Inventory, which confirmed there were zero small tees and found that invoice #501 was blocking Bulldog Print Co. Inventory brought in Accounting to request the $840 invoice payment and an $8 purchase order for one tee, then Customer Service to draft a response to Tauhid. That was a sensible division of work, and the draft didn't promise a delivery date we couldn't guarantee.

The main issue was that I approved both requests while the agents were still running. The invoice was paid and the PO was placed with an expected arrival of September 5, but Boss still marked the ticket waiting_on_approval and said the vendor was blocked. It was using old information from the handoff. The shirt still hadn't arrived, so fulfillment wasn't complete, but the reason given for waiting was no longer accurate.

Ticket 102 was the cleanest. Boss called Facilities, Facilities checked the lease, and Accounting requested the $2,400 rent payment. They correctly identified September 2 as the due date, rather than treating rent as overdue. After I approved it, checking dropped from $2,560 to $160, the next rent date moved to October 2, and the ticket became resolved. They didn't bring in Inventory or Customer Service unnecessarily.

Ticket 103 was mostly good on the numbers and purchasing, but weaker on communication. Inventory found eight medium hoodies and a shortfall of 12. Accounting calculated a price of about $36.67 to preserve the 40% margin floor, and Boss approved that price conditionally on restocking. Boss then brought Accounting back for a separate need: requesting the $264 PO. I approved it, and the database marked the ticket resolved. However, no customer draft was created. Also, resolved here means the approval process was completed; it doesn't mean the hoodies arrived or the customer received them.

2. How Actual compared with Expected

For 101, the delegation chain was pretty close to what I expected: Boss to Inventory, then Inventory to Accounting and Customer Service. The agents also requested an explicit one-unit PO, which my plan hadn't spelled out. The biggest difference was the stale final status after my approvals. I expected Boss to report what was actually still pending.

For 102, the run followed my plan almost exactly. Facilities verified the lease and asked Accounting to queue payment. No landlord draft was created, but I had only expected one if needed. The cash calculation also matched: $3,400 minus the $840 invoice and $2,400 rent left $160.

For 103, I expected Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service. In the latest run, only Inventory and Accounting joined Boss, so the customer communication step was missing. The vendor also wasn't blocked anymore because I had already paid invoice #501 during ticket 101. That showed why the agents needed to check current information instead of assuming the original blocker still applied.

I also need to correct my original cash assumption. I expected the $264 hoodie restock to create an immediate $104 shortfall against the remaining $160. In this app, POs are placed on credit and don't reduce checking. Accounting did flag the potential $104 gap if a later payment were required, but approving the PO itself left cash at $160. The $8 tee PO worked the same way.

One smaller difference across all three was that Boss opened the supplied ticket directly with ticket_dossier_fetch instead of using desk_board_overview first.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

The rent ticket would have been much simpler with one agent. It could check lease_rent_schedule, read cash_position_report, and file the payment request without passing the same facts through Boss, Facilities, and Accounting. The latest rent run used 14 model requests for a fairly straightforward task.

One agent could probably handle much of 101 and 103 too, since stock, vendor blockers, cash, and margins already come from structured tools. Multiple agents made the responsibilities easier to follow, but added handoffs and opportunities for information to get stale, which happened on 101. A single agent would still need to refresh the state after human actions. Human approval should remain separate either way.

4. Three new problems this team could handle with the existing tools

First, another student group could request 30 Yale caps at a discount. The database has 40 caps, with a $6 cost and $22 list price. Inventory could check availability, Accounting could evaluate discounted margins with stock_margin_quote, Boss could decide the price, and Customer Service could draft the offer. Actually recording the sale would be outside the current tools.

Second, a customer could request Crest Mugs, which currently have zero stock. Inventory could find Elm City Gifts through supplier_catalog_search and check its three-day lead time and invoice blockers. Accounting could request a PO for human approval, and Customer Service could draft an availability update, similar to the tee ticket.

Third, the shop could receive another rent notice for the Chapel Street lease. Facilities could compare it with lease_rent_schedule, and Accounting could check cash and existing requests before requesting payment. After the latest run, that would mean recognising the next due date is October 2 and that checking currently has only $160.

5. Three new problems it couldn't handle without additional tools

First, Tauhid could return the tee and request a refund. Customer Service could draft a reply, but the app can't look up a completed sale, confirm the original payment, issue a refund, or put returned stock back into inventory. I would need sales lookup, return, refund-approval, and inventory-adjustment tools, used by Customer Service, Accounting, and Inventory.

Second, Bulldog Print Co could say the 12 hoodies shipped, but only ten arrive. The current app records a PO and expected arrival, but can't track a shipment or record what physically arrived. Inventory would need shipment-tracking and goods-receipt tools, probably with a fulfillment agent to manage discrepancies. This is also why marking 103 resolved doesn't prove the order was fulfilled.

Third, Campus Customs could need extra staff for a busy Yale event. None of the current tools contain employee availability, skills, hours, or payroll information. I would need a staffing agent with scheduling and availability tools, plus Accounting access to wage estimates and payroll approvals. The current inventory and cash tools could provide some context, but they couldn't build or execute a staffing plan.
